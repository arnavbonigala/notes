## Lecture notes

### Course overview
- Math 52 was formerly called Math 1B. It is Calculus II.
- Planned topics, in order:
  1. **Integration techniques.** These cover ordinary definite integrals and also improper integrals, where the interval of integration is infinitely long.
  2. **Chapter 11: Sequences and series.** Sequences are progressions of numbers. Series are sums of infinitely many numbers.
  3. **Power series and Taylor series.**
  4. **Differential equations**, in roughly the last three and a half weeks. This includes some second-order differential equations and some extra material that appears to be series solutions of differential equations. The transcript is unclear here.

## Logistics and exam hints mentioned

### Grading
- Homework (written): 12.5% of the final grade.
- Discussion participation: 12.5% of the final grade.
- Together, homework and discussion make up 25% of the final grade.
- Discussion participation weighting is the one grading policy where this section differs from Dr. Stankova's.
- Participation is graded on effort by the GSIs. Show up and make a good effort, and you get full credit.

### Curve and grade guarantees
- Scores may be scaled in your favor, based on historical grade distributions on BerkeleyTime.
- There are score bins with a guaranteed minimum grade. A score of 95 was mentioned as an example. The exact bins were not given in the transcript.
- The curve only helps you. It will never drag your grade down.

### Homework
- It appears to be due Thursday.
- Late penalty: turning it in Friday instead of Thursday costs 2.5 out of 10 points. The instructor called this a very small penalty.
- Your lowest homework scores are dropped, which covers illness and emergencies. The transcript says "drop your 2.0 scores," which probably means the two lowest scores.
- **Submission:** write your solutions on paper, scan them with your phone (the Gradescope app works) or at the library, and upload them to Gradescope. Uploading a PDF also works.
- GSIs grade homework on completion.

### Exams
- **Only a pencil and an eraser are allowed.** No pens, no calculators, nothing else.
- Since you can't use a calculator, exact answers are fine. Graders are looking at your process, and partial credit is available along the way.
- It's fine to use a calculator while studying. Just remember you won't have it on the exam.

### Discussion sections

### Waitlisted and late-enrolling students
- You're expected to keep up: do the homework and attend discussions.
- Go to the discussion section you're waitlisted for.
